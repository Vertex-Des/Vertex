Learn Desmos, then solve real SAT problems

A quick tour of the tools you'll actually use on the SAT — try them yourself in the calculator below.

Try it live

This is a real, working Desmos calculator. Type an equation, drag a point, add a slider — nothing here is a screenshot.

The tools you need

Graphing equations

Type any equation into an expression line — y=2x+3 — and Desmos plots it instantly. Two equations plotted together show their intersection point, which is the solution to a system of equations.

Sliders

Type an equation with an extra constant, like y=ax+3, and Desmos offers to add a slider for a. Drag it to see how changing that constant reshapes the graph — useful for exploring quadratics and transformations.

Regressions

Add a table (click the + and choose Table), and enter your data — Desmos labels the columns x1 and y1 automatically. Then add a new expression: y1 ~ mx1+b. Desmos fits the best-fit line and reports the values of m and b directly — swap in y1 ~ ax12+bx1+c for a quadratic fit.

Statistics on a list

Put your values straight inside the parentheses, like mean(2,4,4,6,8), median(2,4,4,6,8), or stdev(2,4,4,6,8) — Desmos computes each statistic instantly, no formula required.

Desmos cheat sheet

Copy‑paste-able prompts for the tricks that come up over and over in Practice. Type any of these straight into an expression line.

One rule to know: in any regression, the variable names in your table or list must be x₁ and y₁ (typed as x_1 and y_1 — Desmos turns the 1 into a subscript automatically) — that part is fixed. Every other letter in the equation (constants like a, b, m, c, k) can be whatever you want, uppercase included, as long as it isn't x, y, or r (bare r triggers Desmos's polar mode).

Mean, median, standard deviation

mean(2,4,4,6,8) median(2,4,4,6,8) stdev(2,4,4,6,8)

Put your values straight inside the parentheses — no formulas to remember.

Distance between two points

distance((x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂))

Gives the straight-line distance instantly — perfect for side lengths, radii, and diagonals without touching the distance formula by hand.

Linear regression

y₁ ~ mx₁+b

Enter a table of points, then add this line. Desmos reports m and b directly under Regression Parameters.

Quadratic & exponential regression

y₁ ~ ax₁²+bx₁+c y₁ ~ a·b^x₁

Same idea as linear regression — swap the right side for the shape of the curve you're fitting.

Solve for an unknown constant

10 ~ a(0-8)²+1034

Plug a known point straight into an equation with an unknown constant (here, a) and regress — Desmos solves for it directly, no algebra required.

Bundle several relationships at once

[a,a,c] ~ [230% of b, 60% of c, p% of b]

Stack multiple equal-value relationships into one vector regression when a problem chains several percentages or variables together.

Restrict the domain

y₁ ~ mx₁+b {x₁>-7}

Add curly braces after a regression to keep Desmos from picking points outside where the equation is actually valid.

Area of a polygon

polygon((x₁,y₁),(x₂,y₂),(x₃,y₃))

Feed in the vertices of any shape and Desmos both draws it and can report distances between its corners — handy for area problems on the coordinate plane.
